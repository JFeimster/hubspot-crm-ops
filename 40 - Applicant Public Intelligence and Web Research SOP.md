# 40 - Applicant Public Intelligence and Web Research SOP

## Purpose

Moonshine Capital does not treat an applicant as a form submission with a pulse. Before an applicant contact is added or materially updated, research the person and any identifiable business so the CRM reflects the actual opportunity and the first outreach has a reason to exist.

This SOP governs funding applicants, partner applicants, broker prospects, and reactivation candidates. It supplements—not replaces—the search-first, duplicate-prevention, and approval rules in the HubSpot CRM Ops Playbook.

## Non-Negotiable Rule

When Jason asks to add or update an applicant contact, complete public intelligence research before finalizing the CRM plan, company decision, routing recommendation, or first personalized outreach.

Do not mistake a thin intake for a thin person. An email address, phone number, business name, city, domain, or social handle is often enough to find the surrounding story.

If reliable research cannot be found, record that outcome and proceed with the intake facts. Do not manufacture a business, affiliation, achievement, or personal detail to make the file look more complete.

## What Research Must Accomplish

1. Confirm identity and distinguish same-name candidates.
2. Identify the operating business, related entities, affiliate brands, and useful websites.
3. Identify relevant professional and social profiles.
4. Understand the business model, industry, audience, and current operating context.
5. Surface practical CRM intelligence: location, role, brand, domain, public contacts, business maturity clues, and appropriate route.
6. Surface human context that can make outreach specific, memorable, and genuinely written for this person.
7. Separate verified facts from useful clues and reasonable operating inference.

## Required Workflow

### 1. Assemble the starting identity packet

Collect the available intake and CRM evidence before searching:

- Full name and name variants
- Email address and domain
- Phone number
- City, state, and service area
- Business name, DBA, employer, or website
- Funding request, purpose, and stated urgency
- Existing HubSpot contact, company, deal, note, and task history
- Gmail/Tally/Wix/Notion context
- Provider state, including Giggle, BankBreezy, Plaid, document, or underwriting context

Search HubSpot first. The goal is not to overwrite existing history with a shiny new internet find; it is to connect the evidence.

### 2. Run the research ladder

Use the lightest search that establishes a useful and credible picture, then widen only when the signal justifies it.

| Priority | Source type | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Applicant-provided assets | Domain, intake links, signatures, company names, social handles, documents |
| 2 | Official web properties | Company site, About page, services, team, contact page, blog, product pages |
| 3 | Search results | Person + company, email domain, phone, city, industry, DBA/affiliate names |
| 4 | Professional/social presence | LinkedIn, Facebook business pages, Instagram, X, YouTube, FieldLevel, industry profiles |
| 5 | Public ecosystem sources | Podcasts, interviews, event pages, association profiles, client stories, press, marketplaces |

Useful query patterns:

```text
"First Last" "Business Name"
"First Last" "City State"
"email@domain.com"
"phone number"
"Business Name" website
"Business Name" Facebook
"Business Name" LinkedIn
"First Last" coach | founder | broker | owner | [industry]
```

Search for affiliated or adjacent brands when the person’s role, links, website footer, social bio, or public description indicates a real relationship. Do not turn every shared surname or casual tag into a corporate family tree.

### 3. Resolve the entity before using it

Use at least two matching signals when practical: name + location, name + business, email domain + company site, phone + profile, or role + official property.

Classify findings in the research note:

| Class | Meaning | CRM treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified fact | Directly supported by intake or credible public source | Use in the appropriate field or note |
| Strong clue | Highly likely, but not cleanly confirmed | Note as a clue; do not force a field |
| Operating inference | A useful read of the evidence | Use to shape strategy; label it as inference in the note |
| Writing hook | A relevant cultural, industry, or personality reference | Use naturally in outreach; do not present it as a formal fact |

The point is better judgment, not a courtroom transcript. A good email can say something that feels observant without pretending every line is a sworn affidavit.

### 4. Build the applicant intelligence record

Capture only decision-useful intelligence.

**Use structured fields only for clean matches:**

- Company name and domain
- Website URL
- City/state
- Job title/role
- Industry, when reliable and an existing field is appropriate
- Public phone/email only when it cleanly belongs and differs from existing data

**Use a HubSpot note for broader intelligence:**

```markdown
## Applicant Public Intelligence — [Name] — [YYYY-MM-DD]

### Identity and business
- [Verified identity/business summary]
- [Primary website]
- [Related/affiliate property and relationship]

### Public operating context
- [What the business does, audience, service area, or current project]
- [Relevant professional/social presence]

### CRM and routing read
- Recommended initial intent: [qualification | funding follow-up | Giggle/BankBreezy | partnership | reactivation]
- Company decision: [create | associate existing | do not create yet]
- Provider/process state: [known facts only]

### Outreach hooks
- [Specific reference, theme, language, industry angle, or metaphor territory]

### Evidence and confidence
- Verified: [source + fact]
- Strong clues: [source + clue]
- Inferences: [why the inference is useful]
```

Do not put a research dump into a contact field, deal amount, or company record merely because HubSpot has a box available.

### 5. Decide the first intent before drafting

Research changes the question from “What generic funding email should we send?” to “What is the most useful next move for this person?”

Choose one primary intent:

- **Qualification:** material underwriting facts are missing.
- **Funding follow-up:** a viable request needs movement or missing information.
- **Giggle/BankBreezy routing:** the applicant is in the provider path and must complete the existing Giggle email/Plaid or document step.
- **Business-specific outreach:** the public business context reveals a clearer, more relevant funding conversation.
- **Partnership/strategic opportunity:** the person or entity is better approached as a channel, affiliate, broker, or ecosystem relationship.
- **Reactivation:** prior activity exists, and the current message should reopen a stalled but relevant thread.

The initial intent is a judgment call. It should be explicit in the plan, not hidden inside a generic email.

### 6. Generate outreach from the intelligence

Use the Jason Voice and Applicant Outreach Style Guide and the Google CC-Style Applicant Review and Outreach Prompt.

The message must have:

- One primary action
- One fallback path when genuinely useful
- A clear connection between the researched person/business and the requested action
- A real reason to act now, including negative framing when it creates useful urgency
- A voice that sounds human, sharp, and specific—not like a lender autoresponder wearing a blazer

For Giggle applicants, the action path is the applicant’s existing Giggle email and its Plaid/bank-connection flow. Do not invent a Moonshine or DAC application link when Giggle owns the link.

## Company-Creation Rule

Public research may validate a company, but it does not create an obligation to create one.

Create or associate a company only when an identifiable operating entity is real, relevant to the opportunity, and cleanly attributable to the applicant. Otherwise, retain the intelligence in the note until the relationship is confirmed.

## Minimum Output for Every Applicant Research Pass

Before any state-changing CRM action, provide or prepare:

1. Identity/business findings
2. Recommended contact/company/deal/note/task changes
3. Recommended initial intent and route
4. Research note content
5. Personalized outreach draft, when requested or operationally useful
6. Proposed follow-up state and stop condition

## Quality Standard

The research pass is successful when a future operator can answer, in under a minute:

- Who is this person?
- What are they actually building or operating?
- What entities and public properties matter?
- What funding or relationship path makes sense?
- What happened in the process?
- What should happen next, and why will this message land?
