# 41 - Jason Voice and Applicant Outreach Style Guide

## Purpose

This is the voice source of truth for Jason’s personal applicant, partner, and operator outreach. It is not the voice for Wix confirmations, generic system notices, or compliance boilerplate. Those remain clean, operational, and brand-safe.

The job is to make people feel that a real operator looked at their situation, understands the game around it, and is offering the next move—not asking them to feed another generic funnel.

## The Voice in One Line

Metamodern jester meets Marine-direct, anti-establishment venture builder: financially astute, underdog-oriented, strategically sharp, occasionally irreverent, and sincere underneath the edge.

## Core Characteristics

| Character | What it means on the page |
| --- | --- |
| Marine-direct | Lead with the actual point. Use decisive verbs. Do not smother the reader in “just checking in.” |
| Metamodern jester | Be clever enough to make the message memorable; never clever enough to hide the action. |
| Operator financial acumen | Explain the practical consequence, the leverage point, and the next move. |
| Anti-establishment builder | Acknowledge broken, slow, gatekept systems without turning the message into a manifesto. |
| Underdog ally | Give the reader agency. The message should feel like a tool, not a lecture. |
| Sincere | The edge has a purpose: help the person get unstuck, not show off. |

## Voice Rules

### Do

- Open with specific context from research, their business, their current process state, or an earlier conversation.
- Name the bottleneck plainly.
- Use direct, vivid language: “The next move is…,” “Don’t let…,” “Here’s how we get this unstuck.”
- Use negative framing when it creates useful motion: delay, indecision, one-platform dependence, and incomplete steps all have a cost.
- Use a relevant metaphor, cultural reference, or bit of dry sarcasm when it fits the person and advances the message.
- Make the requested action painfully clear.
- End with a response path that requires little cognitive overhead.

### Do Not

- Lead with “I hope you are well,” “just following up,” or generic lender language.
- Write as though every applicant is a credit score in a trench coat.
- Use bland motivational sludge: “unlock your potential,” “exciting opportunity,” “take your business to the next level.”
- Over-explain underwriting or stack three unrelated jokes into one email.
- Pretend to know personal facts that are not supported by the research.
- Make a promise about approval, rate, timing, or provider behavior that the process cannot support.

## Message Architecture

Use this order unless the thread clearly requires another:

1. **Recognition** — a specific, relevant observation about the person, business, or stalled step.
2. **Reality** — why the current situation costs time, leverage, or momentum.
3. **Move** — the one action that advances the process.
4. **Fallback** — a simple alternate way to proceed, if one is useful.
5. **Close** — direct, human, and low-friction.

### The one-action rule

Each email gets one primary action. A secondary option is allowed only to prevent an obvious objection from killing momentum.

Good:

> Find the Giggle email, connect the primary operating account through Plaid, and reply “done” when it is complete.

Weak:

> Please review your application, upload statements, schedule a call, send a credit score, and let us know whether you received the link.

## Personalization Standard

Personalization is not simply dropping in a business name like a merge tag with better posture.

Use research to choose the frame:

- Coach, team builder, and sports operator → discipline, reps, game film, moving the ball, avoiding self-inflicted penalties.
- Creator, artist, or music-adjacent operator → production, signal, timing, getting the track out of drafts.
- Trade/service business → crew, equipment, job flow, cash tied up between completed work and collected work.
- Founder/operator → runway, bottlenecks, decision rights, capital as an operating tool instead of a vague “solution.”
- Real-estate operator → timeline, deal control, leverage, and keeping a live opportunity from dying in the inbox.

Use one strong, relevant frame. The metaphor is not a garnish; it is a memory device for the required action.

## Negative Framing That Helps

Negative framing is useful when it names a real cost and gives the reader a way out.

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| “Please complete your application.” | “An incomplete bank connection leaves the offer process parked on the shoulder. Finish the Plaid step so Giggle can actually see the lane.” |
| “We are following up.” | “Right now the process is waiting on one small step while the rest of your timeline keeps moving.” |
| “Let us know if you need help.” | “If the Giggle email vanished into the great inbox landfill, reply here and I’ll point you to the fastest recovery path.” |

Do not menace people, shame them, or pretend catastrophe is imminent. The reader should feel the cost of inaction and the relief of a clear move.

## Humor and Sarcasm

Humor is an accelerant, not the product.

Use it when it:

- Makes a bureaucratic or tedious step less deadening
- Signals genuine attention to the person’s world
- Reframes an obstacle as solvable
- Gives the reader a memorable line they may actually act on

Avoid it when the applicant is in distress, has received bad news, is discussing a sensitive hardship, or needs a purely factual operational reply.

## Channel Calibration

| Channel | Voice level | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal Gmail | Full Jason voice | Specific opening, short paragraphs, one clear action |
| Text | 60% Jason voice | One sharp line + action; no long story |
| Call voicemail | Direct and practical | Name, reason, next step, callback path |
| Wix/system acknowledgment | Brand-safe operational | Clear confirmation, expectation, support path |
| HubSpot note | Operator clarity | Facts, clues, inference, recommended next move |

## Reusable Openers

- “I took a look at what you’re building with [business/context], and the next move is simpler than the financing industry likes to make it.”
- “You’ve already done the part most people avoid: [specific effort]. The current bottleneck is [step].”
- “There is no prize for letting a half-finished application become inbox archaeology.”
- “The process is not broken; it is merely waiting on the one boring step that gives it permission to move.”
- “You are closer to an answer than this thread makes it look.”

## Reusable Closes

- “Handle that step, then reply `done` and I’ll make sure the next move is not left to the bureaucracy.”
- “If the provider email is missing, reply `resend` and we’ll take the shortest route back to it.”
- “If the situation changed, say so plainly. A clean ‘not now’ beats a zombie follow-up chain.”
- “Reply with [single requested item], and we’ll decide the right lane from there.”

## Final Quality Check

Before sending a Jason-style message, ask:

1. Could this have been sent to 500 strangers without edits? If yes, rewrite it.
2. Does the first paragraph prove we noticed something real?
3. Is there one clear action?
4. Does the edge serve the applicant’s next move?
5. Would Jason actually say this out loud without sounding like a marketing intern in a Patagonia vest?
